VEGETABLE BIRYANI RECIPE
========================

A fragrant, layered basmati rice dish cooked with spiced vegetables and
saffron-infused milk. Serves 4.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
INGREDIENTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

FOR THE RICE
  • 2 cups (400 g) aged basmati rice
  • 4 cups (1 litre) water
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 4 green cardamom pods
  • 4 cloves
  • 1 cinnamon stick (2 inches)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp ghee or oil

FOR THE VEGETABLE MASALA
  • 3 tbsp ghee or neutral oil
  • 2 medium onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 cup cauliflower florets
  • 1 medium carrot, diced
  • 1 medium potato, diced (1 cm cubes)
  • ½ cup green peas (fresh or frozen)
  • ½ cup French beans, cut into 2 cm pieces
  • ½ cup plain yoghurt (whisked)
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp red chilli powder
  • 1 tsp coriander powder
  • ½ tsp turmeric powder
  • 1 tsp garam masala
  • 1 tsp biryani masala (store-bought or homemade)
  • Salt to taste

FOR THE SAFFRON LAYER
  • A pinch of saffron strands
  • 3 tbsp warm milk

FOR GARNISH & LAYERING
  • 2 tbsp ghee
  • 1 large onion, thinly sliced and fried until golden (birista)
  • 2 tbsp fresh mint leaves, chopped
  • 2 tbsp fresh coriander (cilantro) leaves, chopped
  • 1 tbsp rose water (optional)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
METHOD
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

STEP 1 — SOAK THE RICE
  Wash the basmati rice under cold water until the water runs clear.
  Soak in fresh cold water for 30 minutes, then drain.

STEP 2 — PAR-COOK THE RICE
  Bring 4 cups of water to a boil in a large pot. Add the bay leaf,
  cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, salt and 1 tsp ghee. Add the drained rice
  and cook on medium-high heat for 8–10 minutes, until the rice is
  70–75% cooked (each grain should still have a firm bite at the centre).
  Drain immediately through a colander and set aside.

STEP 3 — FRY THE ONIONS (BIRISTA)
  Heat 2 tbsp ghee in a heavy-bottomed pan over medium heat. Add the
  sliced onions and fry, stirring often, for 15–18 minutes until deep
  golden brown and crisp. Remove with a slotted spoon onto a paper towel.
  Reserve the flavoured ghee in the pan.

STEP 4 — MAKE THE VEGETABLE MASALA
  In the same pan with the reserved ghee, add 1 tbsp more ghee if needed.
  Add cumin seeds and let them splutter (30 seconds).
  Add the ginger-garlic paste and sauté for 1–2 minutes until raw smell
  disappears.
  Add tomatoes and cook on medium heat for 4–5 minutes until soft and
  the oil begins to separate.
  Add red chilli powder, coriander powder, turmeric and salt. Stir well.
  Add all the vegetables (cauliflower, carrot, potato, beans, peas) and
  toss to coat in the masala.
  Reduce heat to low, add the whisked yoghurt a little at a time while
  stirring continuously to prevent curdling.
  Cover and cook for 12–15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the
  vegetables are just tender (not mushy). The masala should be thick and
  clinging to the vegetables.
  Stir in garam masala and biryani masala. Taste and adjust salt.

STEP 5 — PREPARE THE SAFFRON MILK
  Steep the saffron strands in 3 tbsp warm milk for 10 minutes.

STEP 6 — LAYER THE BIRYANI
  Use a heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven with a tight-fitting lid.
  Spread the vegetable masala evenly as the bottom layer.
  Spoon half the par-cooked rice over the vegetables in an even layer.
  Scatter half the fried onions, half the mint and half the coriander
  over the rice.
  Add the remaining rice as the top layer.
  Drizzle the saffron milk over the top.
  Scatter the remaining fried onions, mint and coriander.
  Drizzle 2 tbsp ghee and the rose water (if using) over everything.

STEP 7 — DUM COOKING (SLOW STEAM)
  Cover the pot tightly. If the lid is not snug, seal the edges with
  a strip of dough (atta) or cover with foil before placing the lid.
  Cook on high heat for 3 minutes, then reduce to the lowest possible
  heat and cook for 20–25 minutes. The steam trapped inside will finish
  cooking the rice and meld all the flavours.
  Turn off the heat and let the pot rest, undisturbed, for 10 minutes.

STEP 8 — SERVE
  Open the lid and gently mix from the sides with a wide spatula,
  bringing the vegetable layer up without breaking the rice grains.
  Serve hot with raita (yoghurt with cucumber and cumin), sliced onion
  salad and a wedge of lemon.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
TIPS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  • Aged long-grain basmati gives the best results — shorter or newer
    rice turns mushy during dum.
  • Do not skip the soaking step; it ensures even, fluffy grains.
  • The dum (slow steam) step is non-negotiable for authentic flavour.
  • Swap or add vegetables freely: bell peppers, mushrooms, corn and
    paneer all work well.
  • For a richer version, fry the vegetables lightly in ghee before
    adding them to the masala.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PREP TIME : 30 minutes (plus 30 minutes soaking)
COOK TIME : 50 minutes
TOTAL TIME : ~1 hour 50 minutes
SERVES     : 4
